Load balancing across provider keys (enforcement protocol v5). `CloudAdapter` now polls rules with `X-Traice-Enforcement-Version: 5` and, when the response carries `loadBalance: { version: "load-balance-v1" }`, applies `LOAD_BALANCE` rules. A rule names your provider keys by alias, with weights. For each call a rule matches (on the request actually sent to the provider, after any swap, rollout, or fallback, and its feature), the SDK picks an alias by weight and passes it to the provider callback as `options.keyAlias`, which your callback maps to its own client or key; trAIce never sees keys. When the provider rate-limits a key (HTTP 429 or `ThrottlingException`), the SDK rests it for the provider's `Retry-After` (1 s to 600 s) or the rule's cooldown, and with `onRateLimit: "next_key"` retries the call on a key it has not tried, at most once per key; otherwise the provider error is thrown. Other errors and refusals by trAIce never switch keys. Each attempt goes through the strict cap check, while a rate-limit rule counts the call once. A rule in preview leaves the call unchanged and reports the key it would use. One decision per call reports the key it ended on, the attempts, the keys it left after a rate limit, and the outcome. If load balancing itself fails, the call goes to the provider as if no rule matched and `onEnforcementError` gets operation `load_balance`. `decide()` skips load-balancing rules, and `matchLoadBalanceRule()` and `pickLoadBalanceKey()` follow trAIce, with conformance cases in `load-balance-v1.json`. `getLoadBalanceStats()` reports calls, switches, exhausted calls, and calls per key alias.
